Import & Export Resources

Learn international trade basics and find practical guidance for importing and exporting between Kenya, Africa and global markets.

1. How to Start an Import Business

  1. Identify products with market demand.
  2. Research reliable international suppliers.
  3. Compare supplier prices and shipping costs.
  4. Confirm import permits and regulatory requirements.
  5. Calculate the complete landed cost.
  6. Arrange shipping and customs clearance.
  7. Plan distribution and sales.

2. Essential Import Documents

  • Commercial invoice.
  • Packing list.
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill.
  • Import declaration documents, as applicable.
  • Certificate of Origin, where required.
  • Certificate of Conformity, where applicable.
  • Relevant import permits and licences.
  • Customs entry and supporting documents.

3. Customs Clearance

Customs clearance involves submitting required documentation, correctly classifying goods, assessing applicable taxes and duties, and obtaining customs release. Importers may engage licensed customs agents for assistance.

4. Exporting Goods from Kenya

  1. Identify a suitable export market.
  2. Research destination-country requirements.
  3. Identify buyers and agree on commercial terms.
  4. Prepare invoices and packing documentation.
  5. Obtain applicable export permits and certificates.
  6. Arrange transport, insurance and customs procedures.

5. Understanding Landed Costs

Landed cost is the total cost of getting imported goods to their intended destination.

  • Purchase price.
  • International freight.
  • Insurance.
  • Customs duties.
  • Applicable taxes and levies.
  • Clearing and handling charges.
  • Local transportation.
  • Bank and payment charges.

Basic formula: Total landed cost ÷ quantity = landed cost per unit.

Use the Import Landed Cost Calculator above for a cost estimate.

6. Avoiding Import Business Risks

  • Verify suppliers before making payments.
  • Request samples where practical.
  • Confirm product specifications.
  • Understand international payment terms.
  • Check shipping and insurance arrangements.
  • Confirm regulatory requirements before shipment.

7. Importer Checklist Before Ordering Goods

  • Confirm product demand in your target market.
  • Identify the correct HS code and classification.
  • Check whether goods are restricted or regulated.
  • Confirm permits, licences and standards.
  • Verify the supplier's identity and business history.
  • Get a quotation with specifications.
  • Confirm Incoterms and cost responsibilities.
  • Calculate duties, taxes, freight, clearance and delivery.
  • Confirm payment arrangements and payment risks.
  • Save all transaction records.

8. Export Readiness Checklist

  • Identify a product suitable for international markets.
  • Research buyer demand and competition.
  • Confirm destination-country regulations and standards.
  • Check export permits and certificates.
  • Prepare a professional quotation.
  • Agree currency, payment terms and Incoterms.
  • Prepare invoice and packing list.
  • Arrange freight, insurance and export clearance.
  • Confirm documents required by your buyer.
  • Maintain shipment and payment records.

9. How to Find and Verify Overseas Suppliers

  1. Request registered business name and address.
  2. Check registration details where possible.
  3. Confirm official website and contact details.
  4. Request product specifications and recent images.
  5. Ask for samples where practical.
  6. Request references or verifiable trade history.
  7. Be cautious about pressure to pay immediately.
  8. Confirm bank details independently.
  9. Choose payment terms suitable for the transaction risk.

10. Understanding Incoterms

Incoterms clarify responsibilities between buyer and seller for delivery, costs and risk. Always use the named place and the Incoterms version in the contract.

  • EXW: Goods made available at the seller's premises.
  • FCA: Delivery to the agreed carrier or location.
  • FOB: Seller delivers goods on board the vessel under the sea transport rule.
  • CFR: Seller pays cost and freight to the named destination port.
  • CIF: Seller pays cost, insurance and freight to the named destination port.
  • DAP: Delivery to the agreed destination, ready for unloading.
  • DDP: Seller assumes delivery responsibilities including import clearance and duties under the agreed term.

11. International Shipping Methods

  • Air freight: Often suitable for urgent, lighter or higher-value cargo.
  • Sea freight: Often suitable for bulky shipments or larger quantities.
  • Courier: Useful for small parcels and express shipments.
  • Road freight: Common for regional trade within Africa.

Compare freight cost, transit time, insurance, reliability, handling and destination charges.

12. International Trade Payment Methods

  • Advance payment: Buyer pays before shipment; it can increase buyer risk.
  • Open account: Buyer pays later under agreed credit terms.
  • Documentary collection: Banks handle documents but do not guarantee payment.
  • Letter of credit: Bank payment undertaking subject to compliant documents and credit terms.

For larger transactions, discuss the risks and terms with your bank.

13. International Trade Glossary

  • HS Code: Harmonised commodity classification code.
  • Customs Duty: Duty assessed under applicable customs rules.
  • Freight Forwarder: Service provider arranging cargo movement.
  • Bill of Lading: Sea transport document.
  • Airway Bill: Air cargo transport document.
  • Certificate of Origin: Document certifying goods' origin when required.
  • MOQ: Minimum Order Quantity.
  • Lead Time: Period from order to delivery.
  • Demurrage: Charges that may apply when cargo/container exceeds permitted facility time.

14. Trade Research & Market Selection

  • Compare demand across markets.
  • Research competitors and selling prices.
  • Understand consumer preferences.
  • Review tariffs and market access conditions.
  • Compare logistics routes and costs.
  • Check currency and payment risks.
  • Research destination regulations.

15. Free Official Import & Export Resources

  • InfoTrade Kenya – Trade procedures, product requirements, documents, agencies and permits.
  • KenTrade – Trade facilitation and digital trade services.
  • Kenya Revenue Authority – Customs, tax and revenue information.
  • KEBS – Standards and conformity information.
  • KEPHIS – Plant health and phytosanitary requirements.
MosesDigital Tip: Verify the product's HS classification, applicable regulations, taxes, permits and costs with the relevant official authority before placing an order. Requirements can vary by product and change over time.

Small Business Management

Practical information to help entrepreneurs establish, manage and grow sustainable businesses.

1. Business Planning

A business plan provides direction. Identify your products, target customers, competitors, operating costs, marketing strategy and financial goals.

2. Financial Record Keeping

Maintain accurate records of business income, expenses, purchases, sales and payments. Good records help you understand performance and prepare for tax obligations.

3. Managing Cash Flow

Monitor money entering and leaving your business. Plan payments carefully, control unnecessary spending and maintain adequate operating funds.

4. Pricing Your Products

Consider purchase costs, transport, operating expenses, taxes and desired profit when setting prices. Review prices as costs and market conditions change.

5. Customer Service

Respond promptly, communicate honestly, deliver on promises and address complaints professionally. Good service encourages repeat business.

6. Growing Your Business

  • Understand your target market.
  • Build a reliable customer base.
  • Promote products online.
  • Monitor business performance.
  • Reinvest carefully in growth.
  • Explore new markets and opportunities.

7. Starting a Business on a Small Budget

  1. Start with a product or service people need.
  2. Research potential customers before spending.
  3. Test demand on a small scale.
  4. Separate business funds from personal spending.
  5. Keep daily income and expense records.
  6. Avoid unnecessary debt and costs.
  7. Reinvest a portion of profits when possible.

8. Simple Business Budgeting

Prepare a monthly budget to understand the cost of operating your business.

  • Stock and inventory
  • Rent
  • Transport
  • Internet and communication
  • Marketing
  • Packaging
  • Staff and labour
  • Other operating expenses

9. Inventory Management

  • Record all stock received.
  • Record sales and stock movements.
  • Check inventory regularly.
  • Identify fast- and slow-moving products.
  • Reorder before popular items run out.
  • Investigate missing, damaged or expired goods.

10. Marketing a Small Business for Free

  • Create a WhatsApp Business profile.
  • Showcase products on social media.
  • Post helpful content consistently.
  • Ask satisfied customers for referrals.
  • Use clear product photos and descriptions.
  • Explore relevant free business directories.
  • Encourage repeat customers.

11. Customer Retention

Keeping customers happy can encourage repeat business and referrals.

  • Deliver consistent service.
  • Respond to enquiries promptly.
  • Communicate delivery details and prices clearly.
  • Resolve complaints fairly.
  • Offer affordable loyalty rewards when suitable.
  • Follow up after a sale where appropriate.

12. Business Growth Planning

  • Set monthly sales targets.
  • Monitor actual results.
  • Identify profitable products.
  • Reduce avoidable costs.
  • Seek new customers and markets.
  • Build an emergency reserve.
  • Review your plan quarterly.

13. Business Record-Keeping Checklist

  • Daily sales records
  • Purchase receipts
  • Expense records
  • Customer orders
  • Supplier details
  • Stock records
  • Payment records
  • Invoices
  • Tax and compliance records

14. Common Small Business Mistakes

  • Mixing personal and business funds.
  • Failing to calculate full product costs.
  • Pricing without understanding profit.
  • Buying too much stock before testing demand.
  • Not keeping financial records.
  • Ignoring customer feedback.
  • Growing too fast without sufficient cash flow.
  • Making payments without verifying suppliers.
